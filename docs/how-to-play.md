## The story

Main menu > **Play** > **Story**: The Witcher 3's Gwent as Geralt played it. You start with its Northern Realms starter deck and a leader
of four factions, travel its lands on its own maps (White Orchard, the royal palace in Vizima, Velen and Novigrad, Skellige,
Toussaint), beat its Gwent players for their cards, buy from its innkeepers and merchants, finish its Gwent quests and
tournaments, and collect every card. The story has its own save, its own collection and its own decks: **Play** and
**Multiplayer** keep every card.

- **Cards** come as they do in The Witcher 3. A renowned player's first loss gives you their card (the Bloody Baron's
  Sigismund Dijkstra). A player of no particular renown gives you a random card the first time. An innkeeper or merchant sells
  their cards, each once. A Gwent quest gives a leader when all its steps are done, and a tournament's round gives its
  player's leader. Some cards are found (Brunwich's barn), and Count Monnier gives you the Skellige deck. Nothing can be
  missed: every player stays, and rematches are always there.
- **Crowns**: a win pays crowns (a first win 15, 30 or 50 as the story goes on, a tournament's round 60, a rematch a
  quarter); shops sell for them, and High Stakes asks an entry fee.
- **Opponents** get stronger along the story: White Orchard's and Velen's players play like beginners, Novigrad's and
  Skellige's better and with stronger decks; The Witcher 3's masters of the game (Thaler, Dijkstra, Crach an Craite,
  Olgierd…) and the tournaments play the hardest. A player's sheet shows how well they play. The difficulty in Settings is
  for **Play** only.
- **Regions** open in The Witcher 3's order: Vizima once you beat the scholar at the White Orchard inn, Velen and Novigrad
  once you beat the Nilfgaardian nobleman, Skellige once Gwent: Velen Players is done, Toussaint once you win High Stakes.
  Hearts of Stone's players come to Velen once Gwent: Skellige Style is done. Count Monnier's tournament takes Skellige decks only.

## Your turn

| Action | Desktop | Android |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Select a card from your hand | Click it: it goes to the showcase on the right, with its description, while you choose its place (click its empty slot in the hand to put it back). Keyboard: Left/Right (or A/D) go through the hand, Enter takes the card | Tap it |
| Play the selected card | Click a highlighted row (weather/scorch: the lit weather board, decoy: one of your framed units). The cursor turns gold where the card can go and shows a red "no" where it can't; clicking there plays a warning sound and keeps the card in your hand. Keyboard: Up/Down (or W/S) choose among the places it fits, Enter plays it there (or where it fits, when only one place does); Esc or right-click puts it back | Same, with taps (the warning sound, no cursor) |
| Use your leader | Click the leader card: it leaves its slot for the right, as a card about to be played, shows where its ability acts (a row, a horn slot, the weather board, a discard pile), and its crown glows. Click the crown to use it; its empty slot, Esc, right-click or another card puts it back. Keyboard: X, then X again or Enter. The crown is gold while the ability can still be used this round, grey once used or after passing, faded out for a passive leader (always in effect, never used) | Tap it, then tap the glowing crown. Hold a finger on the crown to see whether it can still be used |
| Pass (for the rest of the round) | Hold Space, or hold the **Hold to pass** button | Hold the button |

## Your battle deck

The deck you last selected in the deck builder (one of yours or a preset) is used in battle, as long as it is valid: at least 22
unit cards, at most 10 special cards, and each card at most as many times as The Witcher 3 has copies of it (heroes and most cards
once, up to 5 Mahakaman Defenders). The line at the bottom of the deck builder shows which deck that is. Without a valid deck, the
battle uses the built-in Northern Realms deck. A deck saved with more copies than that loses the extra ones when it is loaded.

## Replays

A battle you finish can be saved as a replay: **Save replay** on its end screen, against the AI and in a LAN battle on both
devices (the guest's once its fair play check has passed). Replays stay until you delete them. A replay shows the battle from your
side of the board, as you saw it; the switch right of your hand turns the board over, to your opponent's side and hand.

| Action | Desktop | Android |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watch a replay | Main menu > **Replays**, then click it: the newest first, each with who played whom, how it ended and when | Tap it |
| Delete one | The cross beside it, then confirm | Same |
| Play and pause | Space, or the middle button of the bar where the pass button is | The middle button |
| A move forward or back | Right or Left, or the buttons beside the middle one. Forward runs to the next move (a play, a pass, a leader, a redraw, a card taken or discarded, a round's end) and pauses once it has shown; back goes to the start of the move showing, or the one before | The buttons |
| A round forward or back | Shift+Right or Shift+Left, or the outer buttons: back starts the round showing again, or the one before when it has only just started | The outer buttons |
| Speed | Up or Down, or the speed button: 1×, 2× or 4× the game speed | The speed button |
| Switch sides | S, or the button right of your hand: the battle from the other side (its hand shown), where it was | The button |
| Stop watching | Esc > **Stop watching**, back to the replays; at the end, **Watch again** or **Replays** | Back > **Stop watching** |

A replay is the battle played again from its moves. One recorded with an earlier version whose rules or cards play those moves
differently says so instead of showing a battle that never was.

## Settings

Main menu or pause menu > **Settings**:

- **Audio** and **Display**. **Display > Menu size** sizes the menus and dialogs. On a phone they start a size larger (by the
  screen's measured size, a tablet's not), as large as the largest menu size, with a larger × and larger battle corner
  buttons; a smaller menu size still shrinks them.
- **Game > Game speed** (Slow, Normal, Fast) sets how fast cards move, notices pass and the opponent plays.
- **Game > Difficulty** (Easy, Normal, Hard) sets how well the opponent plays (see [The AI](ai.md)).
- **Game > Check for updates** (on by default) looks for a newer version as the game starts. When there is one, the main
  menu says so in its top right corner; it opens what's new and **Update**, which reloads the page in a browser, installs the new
  version on Android (Android asks you to confirm), and on a computer replaces the game's files and restarts it. Settings and
  decks stay.
- **Game > Censored artwork** (on by default) shows covered versions of the cards' art that has them, and of the main menu's
  key art. It switches at once, everywhere on screen, even in the middle of a battle.
- **Graphics** has a preset (Low, Medium, High; Custom once you change one) over how many particles the effects emit, and each
  effect on or off, under Weather, Battle effects, Motion and Menus: animated weather (off: still pictures), the storm's lightning
  and thunder, Clear Weather's sun, the hero's landing, the scorch's fire, the ability flares (off, each keeps a light stand-in: a
  gold edge, the cards charring, the icon), the medic's flames, the horn's glow, the landing wisps, the shine and pulsing, the menu
  animations. A phone starts on Medium, the rest on High.
